1

🧬 From language to action: LLM → VLM → VLA

The progression from models that process only text (LLMs) for models that also see (VLMs) and, finally, for models that generate motor actions in the real world (VLAs). Each leap adds a modality — understanding this chain is the foundation for everything that comes later in the course.

🔑 The three acronyms you’ll never forget

  • LLMLarge Language Model. Processes sequences of text tokens. Text goes in, text comes out. E.g.: GPT-3, LLaMA.
  • VLMVision-Language Model. Add a visual encoder to process images alongside text. E.g.: GPT-4V, LLaVA.
  • VLAVision-Language-Action. Extends the VLM with a action head that decodes tokens into continuous motor commands—position (x,y,z) and gripper rotation.

💡 Why this matters

Without clarity on what changes between GPT-4V and RT-2, it's impossible to evaluate VLA architectures or decide which model to use in a real project. The difference isn't "one more feature"—it's a new output modality: action.

2

🪢 The gap between understanding and acting

There is a chasm between semantic understanding (knowing what a cup is) and motor skill (pick up the cup without dropping it). VLMs understand scenes precisely, but they don't translate that understanding into coordinated physical actions. VLAs exist to close this gap.

✓ What a VLM already handles

  • ✓Recognizing that there is a cup on the table
  • ✓Describe the scene in natural language
  • ✓Answer "where is the cup?"

✗ What’s still missing (and what VLA tackles)

  • ✗Calculate the arm's trajectory to the cup
  • ✗Adjust the gripper force in real time
  • ✗Correct the motion when the cup slips

Three concepts for naming the gap

Grounding

Ground abstract concepts in concrete sensory perceptions.

Sim-to-real gap

Difference between performance in simulation and in the real world.

Closed-loop control

Continuously adjust actions based on sensory feedback.

3

🏗️ Foundation models for robotics

The paradigm of foundation models (large-scale pretraining + fine-tuning) applied to robotics. Instead of training from scratch for each task and robot, you start with a base model trained on massive datasets from multiple robots and environments — and then adapt it. That's why VLAs have become viable.

📊 Why pretraining transfers so well

  • Transfer learning — reuses knowledge from data-rich domains (the internet) for data-scarce domains (robotics).
  • Multimodal pretraining — combines vision and language into a unified representation.
  • Cross-embodiment — a single model controls robots with different morphologies.

💡 Practical tip

Rich representations from the internet transfer surprisingly well to motor control — even with few demonstration data points. In practice: you rarely train a VLA from scratch; you fine-tune a base model.

4

🖐️ Affordances: what the robot CAN do

A concept from J.J. Gibson's ecological psychology adapted for robotics: affordances are the action possibilities that an object or environment offers. A cup "affords" being grasped; a door, being opened. Detecting affordances means identifying which actions are physically feasible in the current scene.

Illustrative diagram — affordance detection distinguishes what is possible from what is merely requested.

Key concepts

  • Visual affordances — map regions of the image to possible actions (graspable surfaces, navigable areas).
  • SayCan (Google) — combines an LLM that proposes actions with a value function that filters the physically feasible ones.
  • TAMP — Task and Motion Planning integrates symbolic planning with continuous motion planning.
5

🤖 Embodied AI vs. Disembodied AI

The distinction between AI that exists only in the digital world (chatbots, image generators) and AI embodied in physical bodies that interact with the real world. Working with VLAs requires a change in mindset: an error isn't a wrong answer in a chat — it's a collision, a dropped object, a safety risk.

Embodied AI

  • •Error = wrong answer, reversible
  • •No physics, no sensory latency
  • •Can "try again" at no cost

Embodied AI

  • •Error = collision, fall, damage—irreversible
  • •Newtonian physics, mechanical wear
  • •Real consequences for every action

Key concepts

Embodiment hypothesis

True intelligence requires a body that interacts with the environment.

Sensorimotor contingencies

Patterns between actions and sensory changes that the agent learns.

Reality gap

The gap between simulation and the real world makes deployment difficult.

6

📅 Timeline: from GPT to autonomous humanoids

In just 3 years we went from "LLMs don't control robots" to "general-purpose VLAs control multiple embodiments." Knowing the timeline helps calibrate expectations and project where we'll be in 2027–2028.

2020

GPT-3 and the LLM era

Large language models exhibit emergent capabilities. The idea of a "foundation model" is born.

2023

RT-2 (Google DeepMind)

First prominent VLA: transfers web knowledge to real robotic control. The action becomes "just another token."

2024

OpenVLA, π0, and Physical Intelligence

Open-source democratizes access. Physical Intelligence (π0) emerges. LeRobot and Octo open up the ecosystem.

2026

Autonomous humanoids

Figure 02, GR00T, and the explosion of robotics startups. General-purpose VLAs control multiple embodiments.

💡 Concepts to follow along the learning curve

Scaling laws investigate whether more data/parameters improve motor performance as they do language. Emergent capabilities are abilities that emerge with scale without being explicitly trained. The ecosystem open-source democratizes what once required labs with millions invested in hardware.
